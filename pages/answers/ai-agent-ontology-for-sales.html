What is an AI agent ontology — and how to build yours in an afternoon. Palantir built their moat on this one idea. Here is the founder-sized version.

By Simon Severino, founder of Strategy Sprints — 199 AI skills, 15 scheduled agents, one Firm Map.

If you are running fewer than 3 AI agents, come back in 90 days. If you are running 3 or more with no shared map, keep reading — this is the fix.

An AI agent ontology is a structured map of your business — objects, actions, rules — that tells every AI agent what a deal is, who a prospect is, and what it is allowed to do.

It has three layers. Objects (nouns): the things your business runs on — prospect, deal, member, partner, recording, offer. Actions (verbs): what is allowed to change those objects — create a deal, send an offer, advance a stage, log a result. Rules: who and what can see and do what.

Every AI agent reads this map before it acts. It stops guessing your definitions and starts operating on yours.

Your AI agents are not underperforming because the model is weak. They are not underperforming because your prompts are sloppy. They are not underperforming because you bought the wrong tool.

That is not a model problem. That is a map problem.

The Three Layers of an AI Agent Ontology

Layer 1: Objects (Nouns)

The things your business actually runs on. For a B2B sales operation, this typically means:

  • Prospect — someone in active outreach who has not yet agreed to a conversation
  • Deal — a prospect who accepted a call; enters at 10%, Initial Call Scheduled
  • Member — a paying subscriber (Sprint Club, $49/month)
  • Partner — a referral relationship; never a prospect, ever
  • Recording — a raw video or audio file pending processing
  • Offer — a priced deliverable (Workshop $5K, 90-Day $27K, 200K Club $900/mo)

Each object points to the one system where it lives — CRM, spreadsheet, inbox. One line each. Every agent reads these definitions before it acts.

Layer 2: Actions (Verbs)

What is allowed to change those objects. For each verb: one owner, one quality gate.

Action Owner Quality Gate
Create a deal Prospecting agent or founder Prospect must have replied; status flips to "Moved to Deals"
Send an offer Closing agent or founder Ogilvy quality check passed; founder approved
Advance a stage Founder only Previous stage criteria met and logged
Add to outreach sequence Prospecting agent Object type = Prospect, not Partner or Member
Publish content Publishing agent SEO title approved; 4-skill quality chain passed

If two agents can both create a deal without a gate, you do not have a process. You have a coin flip.

Layer 3: Rules

Who and what can see and do what. In Palantir's framing: what humans cannot see, AI cannot see. In founder terms:

  • Partners are never contacted as prospects — hard rule, no exceptions
  • Offer prices are never disclosed in first outreach
  • A deal enters at exactly 10%, Initial Call Scheduled — no other door exists
  • Content does not publish without an SEO-optimized title
  • No URL ships in outgoing content unless it passed the URL validator

These rules are not reminders. They are gate conditions in the code and in the map. An agent that cannot pass the gate cannot take the action.

How to Build Yours in One Afternoon

Step 1 — Name Your Nouns (1 hour)

Block one hour. List the 10 to 15 objects your business actually runs on. For each one, write: what it is, how it becomes that thing, and which single system it lives in.

Example: Deal — a prospect who accepted a discovery call. Lives in HubSpot. Enters at 10%, Initial Call Scheduled. Exits at Closed Won or Closed Lost.

You already know most of these by heart. The hour is for writing them down where your agents can read them.

Step 2 — Govern Your Verbs (1 hour)

List every action that changes those objects. For each verb: one owner (a named agent or the founder), one quality gate (the condition that must be true before the action runs).

The test: if two tools can both perform this action without a gate, it is not governed. Pick one system. Block the others.

Step 3 — Wire the Loop (30 minutes)

Every action leaves a trace — a log line, a timestamp, an outcome. Read the trace every Friday and double down on what worked. Update the map when the business changes.

This is Monday Strategy and Friday Learnings applied to your machines. The loop keeps the map fresh — nothing goes stale without you noticing.

One rule change used to mean 199 edits — one per skill. Now it means one edit, in one file, and 199 skills inherit it automatically.

The Compounding Effect Nobody Talks About

The first AI agent you build without a Firm Map takes weeks. The second takes weeks too — because you are re-explaining the entire business from scratch inside every agent's context.

With a Firm Map, the first agent takes weeks. The second takes hours. The tenth takes thirty minutes. Every future agent inherits the complete business definition on day one.

Strategy Sprints runs 199 AI skills and 15 scheduled agents. Before we wrote our Firm Map, every one of them carried its own private definition of a deal. One rule change meant 199 edits. A new agent meant re-explaining the whole firm from zero.

We wrote the map in one afternoon. We have not re-explained the firm to a new agent since.

What Makes an Ontology Different from a Prompt or a Context File

A prompt tells an agent what to do in one session. A context file tells an agent about your business for one task. An ontology tells every agent what the firm is — permanently, centrally, authoritatively.

Type Scope Durability Compounding
Prompt One task, one session Forgotten after session None
Context file One agent, many sessions Persists for that agent Low — must be copied per agent
Ontology (Firm Map) All agents, all sessions Permanent, centrally maintained High — all future agents inherit automatically

What Happens When You Skip the Map

The agents still run. Output looks reasonable in weeks one and two. By week four you are seeing inconsistencies — one agent calls a referral partner a prospect, another emails a current member a cold pitch, a third quotes a price that changed three weeks ago.

Most founders diagnose this as an AI problem and turn the agents off. The real diagnosis: no shared definition of the firm.

A brilliant prompt on top of an unmapped business is a brilliant intern with no onboarding — confident, fast, and wrong.
